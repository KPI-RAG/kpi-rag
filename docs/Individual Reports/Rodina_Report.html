5.W.1 Overview and Standards Mapping Protocol

This section establishes the normative ground-truth alignment between operational fault categories observed in 5G New Radio (NR) access networks and 3GPP Release 19 Technical Specifications alongside O-RAN Alliance architectural standards. The alignment matrix provides the domain-knowledge foundation for the KPI-RAG retrieval and explanation engine, mapping high-dimensional KPI metric anomalies to physical fault mechanisms.

The taxonomy encompasses 11 distinct fault classes: 10 synthetic telecom time-series fault profiles generated from the C3 dataset (covering hardware, software, usage, and environmental domains) and 1 over-the-air (OTA) RF jamming scenario validated on physical testbed equipment. Each of the 10 synthetic fault classes is mapped to its governing 3GPP specification clause, affected O-RAN logical component (O-RU, O-DU, or Near-RT RIC), physical causal mechanism, and primary SHAP feature attributions extracted from Layer 2 tree-ensemble models.

5.W.3 Comprehensive Analysis of Causal Mechanisms and Normative Mapping

The v1.2 normative mapping directly couples physical transmission phenomena and MAC/RLC protocol dynamics to standard KPI telemetry signatures across O-RAN functional splits. Below is a detailed technical analysis across all 11 fault classes organized into functional operational groups:

Group 1: Physical Radio Front-End & Antenna Failures (#1, #7)

Antenna Failure (#1): Physical antenna degradation or connector disconnection reduces effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) and receiver sensitivity at the O-RU antenna port A (governed by 3GPP TS 38.141-1 §4.2.1 and §7.2 PREFSENS limits). The physical signal attenuation manifests as a sharp drop in Reference Signal Received Power (RSRP). To maintain target Block Error Rate (DL_BLER), the gNB's Link Adaptation / AMC algorithm drops the Downlink Modulation and Coding Scheme (DL_MCS) to robust QPSK constellations, leading to a severe throughput collapse.

Faulty RF Filters (Temporal) (#7): Thermal or mechanical degradation of receiver bandpass filters at the O-RU allows out-of-band emissions to bleed into active carrier bandwidths. Under TS 38.141-1 §7.5 (Out-of-band blocking) and §7.6 (Receiver spurious emissions), filter failure raises the receiver thermal noise floor. This gradually degrades Uplink Signal-to-Noise Ratio (UL_SNR) and forces the MAC scheduler to assign extra Uplink PRBs (PRBs_UL_Current) to compensate for retransmissions.

Group 2: MAC Scheduler, Protocol & Queueing Faults (#2, #10)

Buffer Overflow (Gradual Buildup) (#2): Occurs when uplink packet arrival rate at the RLC layer exceeds the gNB scheduler's grant allocation rate. Governed by TS 38.321 §6.1.3.1 (BSR MAC Control Elements) and §5.4.5 (Buffer Status Reporting procedures), the UE repeatedly issues Regular BSRs indicating high queue occupancy. Insufficient O-DU scheduling grants cause the RLC buffer to saturate, raising PRB_Utilization_UL to 100% while triggering packet drops and latency spikes.

Resource Allocation Bugs (#10): Software defects in the O-DU MAC scheduler corrupt the token-bucket state variable $B_j$ during Logical Channel Prioritization (LCP), governed by TS 38.321 §5.4.3.1.3. Instead of allocating resources based on Prioritized Bit Rate (PBR) and LCG priority, the scheduler assigns excessive Downlink PRBs (PRBs_DL_Current) to specific bearers while starving others, creating extreme variance in PRBs_DL_Current_max without proportional throughput increases.

Group 3: Interference & Over-The-Air RF Anomalies (#3, #4, #11)

Co-Channel Interference - Mild (#3) & Severe (#4): Uncoordinated neighbor cell transmissions on overlapping resource blocks elevate the co-channel interference power density. Governed by TS 38.141-1 §7.8 (In-channel selectivity) and TS 38.104 §4.9 (Base station co-location requirements), mild interference (#3) causes a moderate UL_SNR drop while RSRP remains unchanged. Severe interference (#4) completely swamps the receiver, causing UL_SNR collapse, DL_BLER spikes above decoding thresholds, and unrecoverable packet loss at the O-RU.

Jamming (OTA Real-world) (#11): Active over-the-air intentional RF jamming emits high-power wideband noise across active carrier frequencies. Unlike multi-path fading, jamming simultaneously suppresses RSRP and causes 100% DL_BLER, collapsing transmitted payload (TX_Bytes). As an external real-world testbed anomaly, it is evaluated against operational ticket logs rather than standardized 3GPP test clauses or O-RAN logical component targets.

Group 4: Environmental & Mobility Channels (#5, #6)

Doppler Shift (Severe) (#5): High UE velocity relative to the gNB induces a time-varying Doppler frequency shift $f_d$:

$$f_d = \frac{v}{\lambda} \cos\theta$$
  • $f_d$: Time-varying Doppler frequency shift ($\text{Hz}$).
  • $v$: Relative velocity of the User Equipment (UE) with respect to the gNodeB (gNB) ($\text{m/s}$).
  • $\lambda$: Carrier wavelength ($\text{m}$), defined as $\lambda = \frac{c}{f_c}$ where $c$ is the speed of light and $f_c$ is the carrier frequency.
  • $\theta$: Angle between the UE's velocity vector and the line-of-sight signal propagation path to the gNB antenna.

Modeled under ETSI TR 38.901 §7.6.6 and §7.6.10 (Dual Mobility), rapid phase variation violates the channel coherence time, corrupting demodulation reference signal (DMRS) channel estimation at the O-RU/O-DU. The AMC scheduler reacts by driving conservative DL_MCS fallbacks and expanding uplink grant allocations (UL_NPRB).

Faulty Handover Algorithm (#6): Sub-optimal mobility parameter configuration (e.g., small a3-Offset or insufficient timeToTrigger) in the Near-RT RIC xApp triggers rapid, cyclic UDM ping-pong handovers between adjacent cells per TS 38.331 §5.5.4.4 (Event A3) and §5.5.4.1. This causes periodic RSRP fluctuations, control-plane signaling storms, and transient throughput drops during RRC re-configuration.

Group 5: Traffic Congestion Dynamics (#8, #9)

High Network Congestion - Gradual (#8) vs. Sudden Spike (#9): Monitored via TS 28.552 §5.1.1.2.1 (RRU.PrbDl) and §5.1.1.2.2 (RRU.PrbUl) performance measurements at the O-DU. Gradual congestion (#8) exhibits a steady upward trend in PRB demand, driving PRB_Utilization_UL toward 100% while UL_SNR slowly degrades due to increased intra-cell inter-user interference. Sudden spike congestion (#9) represents an instantaneous burst of user arrivals, causing immediate PRB exhaustion, DL_BLER transient spikes, and UL_SNR degradation within a single evaluation window.

Finding 5.W.1 — Standards Alignment Completeness
Ten operational fault classes are mapped to normative 3GPP Release 19 clauses and O-RAN component boundaries (with real-world OTA Jamming evaluated via physical testbed logs without standardized 3GPP clauses or internal O-RAN target assignments). The top SHAP feature attributions align with physical-layer radio propagation and protocol-layer behaviors specified in TS 38.141-1, TS 38.321, and TS 38.331.
Section 5.X · Results and Evaluation

Track D: TeleQnA Benchmark Evaluation

TeleQnA Benchmark (600 questions) · Baseline LLM vs. KPI-RAG System · Retrieval coverage audit with confidence-gate analysis

5.X.1 Overview and Experimental Setup

Track D evaluates KPI-RAG on the TeleQnA multiple-choice benchmark [Maatouk et al., 2023], a publicly available dataset of telecommunications domain questions derived from 3GPP standards documentation. The evaluation is designed to characterise the system's knowledge scope boundary: KPI-RAG was developed for operational fault diagnosis in 5G networks, and TeleQnA probes an adjacent but distinct domain—standards specifications and lexicon definitions.

The evaluation draws 200 questions from each of three TeleQnA categories: Standards specifications, Standards overview, and Lexicon, yielding 600 candidate questions in total. Two categories from the original benchmark—Research overview and Research publications—are excluded, as they require reasoning over scientific literature that the KPI-RAG evidence base was not designed to support.

Two evaluation conditions are tested on the same question set under a pre-registered, two-stage protocol:

  • Baseline LLM — the language model answers each question with no retrieved context, relying solely on parametric knowledge acquired during pre-training.
  • KPI-RAG — the retriever queries the ChromaDB knowledge base (935 fault-diagnosis telemetry tickets) and the top-k chunks are prepended to the prompt before the LLM answers.

Stage 1 of the protocol performs a coverage audit: every question is passed through the retriever and flagged as supported if the maximum cosine similarity score across the top-k retrieved chunks meets or exceeds the strict coverage threshold of $\tau = 0.50$ (an internal low-confidence log flag is recorded at $\tau = 0.35$). In the raw pipeline execution, retrieved chunks are prepended for all questions regardless of coverage status to measure the effect of ungated context injection.

5.X.4 Analysis: Context Poisoning from Domain Mismatch

The −24.5 pp degradation from baseline to KPI-RAG is explained by a context poisoning effect [Lewis et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2023]. When the retriever cannot find relevant evidence, the ungated RAG pipeline still injects the top-k chunks—which in this case are fault-diagnosis narratives describing co-channel interference, jamming events, and congestion buildup—as context for a standards or lexicon question. The language model, conditioned on this irrelevant context, partially overrides its correct parametric knowledge with false signals from the injected noise.

Manual inspection of retrieved evidence for representative questions confirms this pattern. In each case the retrieved chunks contain operationally-plausible telemetry language but are semantically unrelated to the question being answered:

Q (q00021): "What is used to initialize the scrambling sequence generator for UE-specific search space in PDCCH?"
↳ Expected: pdcch-DMRS-ScramblingID (3GPP TS 38.211)

Retrieved chunk (score = 0.217): "Issue: Severe communication degradation due to external RF jamming detected on sector 4B. Interference power: −72 dBm..."

Baseline → correct (option 4). KPI-RAG → wrong (option 1: C-RNTI).

The effect magnitude varies by category in a way that is consistent with the poisoning hypothesis. Lexicon suffers the largest drop (−45.5 pp) because vocabulary definition questions test knowledge that the pre-trained LLM has encoded with high confidence; injected irrelevant context is sufficient to displace the correct answer. Standards specifications shows the smallest drop (−6.0 pp) because those questions are harder for the baseline model too, reducing the marginal cost of noise, and because some retrieved fault-diagnosis chunks contain incidental technical terminology (channel codes, frequency bands) that provides weak but non-zero signal.

Finding 5.X.1 The KPI-RAG evidence base covers 2.17% of TeleQnA questions at a 0.50 cosine similarity threshold. Injecting low-confidence, domain-mismatched context degrades overall accuracy by 24.5 percentage points relative to a no-context baseline, with the largest penalty in the Lexicon category (−45.5 pp). This result confirms that RAG systems require domain-aligned evidence to improve over parametric baselines.

The sweep demonstrates a monotonic recovery: as $\tau$ increases, fewer questions receive RAG context, and accuracy rises steadily toward the baseline. Finding 5.X.2 A retrieval confidence gate at $\tau = 0.50$ eliminates 98.8% of the accuracy degradation attributable to context poisoning, reducing the gap from −24.5 pp to −0.3 pp versus the no-context baseline. At $\tau = 0.55$, full baseline parity is recovered. This result motivates a confidence gate as a zero-cost mitigation strategy for RAG systems deployed outside their primary evidence domain.

5.X.6 Discussion

The Track D results should be interpreted as a scope characterisation rather than a performance failure. KPI-RAG was designed and optimised for a specific operational task: given a set of degraded network KPIs, retrieve relevant fault-diagnosis precedents and produce an explainable root-cause hypothesis. The TeleQnA benchmark tests a categorically different capability—recall and reasoning over 3GPP standards text and telecom terminology—for which the system's evidence base provides no coverage.

In this respect, the low retrieval scores (median < 0.31 across 600 questions) are not a retrieval failure but a correct self-assessment: the system accurately identifies that it has no relevant evidence for standards QA questions. The degradation arises from the absence of a confidence gate in the evaluation pipeline, which forces injection of irrelevant context regardless of retrieval confidence. The threshold sweep in Section 5.X.5 demonstrates that this is fully correctable through a simple post-hoc filter with no retraining or additional data collection.

The result also has a methodological implication for RAG evaluation more broadly. Reporting a single accuracy figure for a RAG system applied to an out-of-domain benchmark without controlling for retrieval confidence can substantially underestimate the system's in-domain performance potential. The 24.5 pp degradation observed here is entirely attributable to the absence of a confidence gate, not to any deficiency in the retriever or generator components on their primary task.

Section 5.Y · Results and Evaluation

Track B: Explanation Quality — LLM-as-Judge (G-Eval)

30 fault windows · 10 fault types · 4 evaluation dimensions · G-Eval framework

5.Y.1 Overview and Evaluation Framework

Track B measures the quality of KPI-RAG's natural language explanations using an automated LLM-as-judge protocol based on the G-Eval framework [Liu et al., 2023]. Human annotation of explanation quality is expensive and difficult to scale; G-Eval has been shown to correlate strongly with expert human judgments on generation tasks when provided with detailed scoring rubrics and chain-of-thought prompting [Gao et al., 2023].

The evaluation covers 30 fault windows drawn from the C3 dataset, with 3 windows sampled per fault type across 10 distinct fault categories. For each window, the KPI-RAG explanation is scored against a structured ground-truth reference on four dimensions:

  • Citation validity — Does the explanation cite a real, applicable 3GPP Technical Specification? Is the cited standard the correct one for the described fault mechanism?
  • Fault specificity — Does the explanation correctly identify the fault family and primary mechanism, linking root cause to the specific KPIs flagged by the SHAP analysis?
  • Actionability — Does the recommended remediation action name the correct O-RAN component and provide sufficient technical specificity for an engineer to act on it?
  • Causal soundness — Is the cause → symptom → KPI chain logically complete and technically accurate, consistent with 5G NR physical-layer and protocol-layer behaviour?

Each dimension is scored 1–5 using a five-point rubric calibrated against expert-written ground-truth explanations. A composite score is the unweighted mean of the four dimensions. Zero inference errors were encountered across all 30 windows.

5.Y.4 Systematic Patterns in Judge Feedback

Consistent Strengths

Across all 10 fault types, the judge consistently awards credit for correctly identifying the primary fault mechanism (e.g., co-channel interference → UL SNR collapse → throughput drop). The system reliably names the correct O-RAN component (O-RU, O-DU MAC, Near-RT RIC) and produces a directionally accurate cause-to-KPI narrative that reflects physical-layer 5G NR behaviour.

Consistent Gaps

Three systematic weaknesses appear in the judge feedback across fault types:

  1. Clause-level citation precision: Every window citing the correct TS scores 4/5 rather than 5/5 because the specific clause is omitted (e.g., §7.8 for TS 38.141-1 CCI selectivity; §5.4.3.1.3 for TS 38.321 MAC LCP). The system identifies the right document but not the normative section.
  2. Incomplete intermediate symptom chain: Explanations consistently skip one layer in the physical-layer chain — typically the BLER rise and MCS floor that bridge the primary fault to the reported throughput KPI. Antenna Failure explanations link degraded antenna → low RSRP but omit the consequent BLER rise that forces the AMC to select a lower MCS before throughput collapses.
  3. Under-parameterised actionability: Remediation actions correctly identify the subsystem and configuration area (MAC scheduler, PCI planning, BSR timer) but omit specific parameter names, threshold values, or target ranges — the largest single opportunity for improvement.
Finding 5.Y.1 — Explanation quality
KPI-RAG achieves a composite G-Eval score of 3.59 / 5.0 (SD = 0.28) across 30 fault windows. The system consistently identifies the correct fault family, names the affected O-RAN component, and establishes a directionally correct causal chain. Three systematic gaps — missing clause-level TS precision, incomplete intermediate symptom chains, and under-parameterised remediation actions — account for the distance from a perfect score and are tractable targets for future work.

5.Y.6 Discussion

A composite score of 3.59 / 5.0 (SD = 0.28) reflects competent, engineer-actionable decision support. Low variance confirms a systematic quality ceiling rather than erratic behavior, driven by three structural gaps (clause precision, intermediate BLER/MCS steps, and parameter values) rather than model failures.
