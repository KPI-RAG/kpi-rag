## Abstract

Modern 5G Radio Access Networks (RAN) generate complex, high-velocity telemetric streams across dozens of Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), making manual root-cause analysis (RCA) and standards verification in Network Operations Centers (NOC) time-consuming and prone to diagnostic error. This report presents **KPI-RAG**, an end-to-end framework for explainable 5G fault diagnosis that bridges data-driven machine learning with domain knowledge from 3GPP technical specifications. Operating on the TelecomTS telemetric dataset (32,000 session-split windows across 18 radio and transport KPIs), the system employs a two-stage CatBoost cascade: a binary anomaly detector (Stage 1, 64 features, 200 trees) achieving **89.7% binary recall** (266/300 true faults detected with 69.6% precision), followed by an 11-class fault classifier (Stage 2, 496 temporal and spectral features, 1,000 trees) delivering **66.3% exact fault accuracy** and **75.0% fault-family accuracy**. For explainability, local SHAP attribution identifies the top-3 influential KPIs per window, which guide cosine-metric dense retrieval over 935 historical troubleshooting tickets in ChromaDB (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`). Crucially, diagnostic explanations generated by Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite are grounded in an authoritative 3GPP Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table and per-window RCA evidence. In a rigorous 30-window, 3-condition ablation study (Track C), intrinsic LLM knowledge alone achieved **27% citation accuracy** (C1), which was not significantly enhanced by historical tickets alone at **23%** (C2); however, full standards-guided prompting (C3) reached **100% citation accuracy** (30/30, 0 template fallbacks), demonstrating a statistically significant grounding delta of **+77 percentage points** ($\Delta C_2 \to C_3$). The complete system is verified across a 175-test automated regression suite and operationalized via a live 6-panel interactive Streamlit dashboard.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background
The deployment of Fifth Generation (5G) New Radio (NR) networks introduces disaggregated architectures (Open RAN), dynamic beamforming, ultra-dense small cells, and network slicing. While these architectural paradigms offer unprecedented spectral efficiency and ultra-low latency, they drastically increase the operational complexity of the radio access and transport networks. Modern Network Operations Centers (NOCs) monitor massive volumes of telemetric Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) generated across physical, medium access control (MAC), radio resource management (RRM), and transport layers. 

When anomalous degradation occurs, identifying the underlying root cause and initiating standard-compliant remediation is critical. The Mean Time to Repair (MTTR) directly dictates service-level agreement (SLA) penalties and user quality of experience (QoE). 

### 1.2 Problem Statement
In traditional operational workflows, when a telemetric window flags an anomaly, NOC engineers are confronted with high-dimensional time-series data across 18 simultaneous KPI channels. To diagnose and remediate the issue, engineers must manually cross-reference observed metric patterns (e.g., severe SINR degradation coupled with rising block error rates) with historical incident logs and authoritative 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) specifications. 

Standard telecom specifications comprise tens of thousands of pages distributed across hundreds of technical specifications (TS) and technical reports (TR). As a consequence:
1. Diagnosis is highly labor-intensive and slow, leading to elevated MTTR.
2. Troubleshooting decisions often lack formal technical auditability and standards compliance.
3. Conventional machine learning classifiers act as black boxes—outputting categorical fault labels without mechanistic justifications or actionable, verified remediation steps.
4. Generative AI models applied naively to telecom telemetries hallucinate standards citations and generate generic, ungrounded troubleshooting advice.

### 1.3 Objectives
The primary objective of the KPI-RAG project is to design, implement, and evaluate an explainable root-cause analysis pipeline that transforms raw 5G time-series telemetries into auditable, standards-grounded operational diagnoses:
1. **Automated Fault Detection and Classification:** Accurately detect anomalies and classify them across 11 diverse telecom fault types using a hierarchical, leak-free ML cascade.
2. **Feature-Attribution Guidance:** Extract local, window-specific feature attributions via SHAP to determine exactly which KPI channels drove the model's prediction.
3. **Retrieval-Augmented Incident Grounding:** Construct vector representations of incident signatures to retrieve relevant historical troubleshooting tickets from a curated vector database.
4. **Verifiable 3GPP Standards Grounding:** Ground LLM reasoning in an authoritative Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table, ensuring that generated explanations cite valid, clause-specific 3GPP specifications and identify the responsible O-RAN architectural component.
5. **Rigorous Empirical Validation:** Conduct structured ablation experiments to quantify the precise marginal contribution of standards grounding over ungrounded baselines.

### 1.4 Research Questions
- **RQ1:** Does conditioning a Large Language Model on SHAP feature attributions and retrieved historical tickets improve citation accuracy and diagnostic actionability compared to an ungrounded LLM?
- **RQ2:** Can explicit grounding via a 3GPP Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table eliminate hallucinated specifications and achieve 100% clause validity in structured diagnostic reports?
- **RQ3:** Does the generated root-cause explanation meet operational requirements for causal soundness, fault specificity, and technical actionability as assessed by domain rubrics?

### 1.5 Report Organization
The remainder of this report is structured as follows: Chapter 2 reviews the theoretical foundations of 5G KPIs, O-RAN, RAG, SHAP, and LLM-as-a-judge evaluation frameworks. Chapter 3 details the methodology, dataset characteristics, feature engineering, and experimental protocols. Chapter 4 presents the end-to-end design, implementation architecture, and software engineering decisions. Chapter 5 reports empirical results across Track A (ML performance), Track C (ablation study), and retrieval metrics. Chapter 6 discusses findings, engineering tradeoffs, failure modes, and practical implications. Chapter 7 concludes the report with future research directions.

## 2. Background and Literature Review

### 2.1 5G Network Architecture and O-RAN
The 3GPP 5G New Radio (NR) architecture decouples the legacy monolithic base station (gNodeB) into distinct physical and logical entities. The Open RAN (O-RAN) Alliance further standardizes open, interoperable interfaces:
- **O-RU (Open Radio Unit):** Processes RF front-end circuitry, digital beamforming, and lower physical layer (Low-PHY) functions. It connects to the O-DU via the Open Fronthaul (WG4) interface.
- **O-DU (Open Distributed Unit):** Implements High-PHY, Medium Access Control (MAC), and Radio Link Control (RLC) protocols, hosting dynamic schedulers and buffer management.
- **O-CU (Open Centralized Unit):** Encompasses the Packet Data Convergence Protocol (PDCP), Service Data Adaptation Protocol (SDAP), and Radio Resource Control (RRC) layers.
- **Near-RT RIC (Near-Real-Time RAN Intelligent Controller):** Hosts microservices (xApps) executing control loops between 10ms and 1s for mobility management, traffic steering, and radio resource allocation.

### 2.2 Key Performance Indicators in 5G
Network observability relies on telemetric channels sampled over consecutive transmission time intervals. The TelecomTS dataset records 18 KPI channels:
1. **Physical Layer KPIs:** Reference Signal Received Power (`RSRP`), Reference Signal Received Quality (`RSRQ`), Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (`SINR`), Downlink and Uplink Signal-to-Noise Ratio (`DL_SNR`, `UL_SNR`).
2. **Error & Reliability KPIs:** Downlink Block Error Rate (`DL_BLER`), Uplink Block Error Rate (`UL_BLER`).
3. **Throughput & Scheduling KPIs:** Downlink Modulation and Coding Scheme (`DL_MCS`), Uplink Modulation and Coding Scheme (`UL_MCS`), Downlink Physical Resource Block Utilization (`PRB_Utilization_DL`), Uplink Physical Resource Block Utilization (`PRB_Utilization_UL`).
4. **Traffic & Transport KPIs:** Transmitted Bytes (`TX_Bytes`), Received Bytes (`RX_Bytes`), Estimated Uplink Buffer (`Estimated_UL_Buffer`), Round-Trip Time (`RTT`), Latency, Jitter, and Protocol state (UDP/TCP).

### 2.3 Fault Types in TelecomTS
The dataset defines 11 distinct fault classes representing physical, protocol, and architectural anomalies:
1. **Antenna Failure:** Physical degradation of transceiver branches or feeder cables, localized at the O-RU, causing catastrophic drops in RSRP/SINR.
2. **Buffer Overflow (Gradual Buildup):** Queue saturation at the O-DU RLC/MAC layer resulting in escalating uplink buffer estimates and packet drops.
3. **Co-Channel Interference (Mild):** Spectral overlap from neighboring cells causing moderate SINR and BLER degradation without complete link failure.
4. **Co-Channel Interference (Severe):** Intense co-channel spectral pollution severely degrading in-channel selectivity and triggering modulation order collapse.
5. **Doppler Shift (Severe):** Rapid carrier frequency offset caused by high-velocity user mobility, impairing subcarrier orthogonality and channel estimation.
6. **Faulty Handover Algorithm (Too Frequent):** Ping-pong handover instability governed by sub-optimal RRC measurement thresholds at the Near-RT RIC.
7. **Faulty RF Filters (Temporal):** Transient hardware filtering anomalies leading to out-of-band emissions and elevated receiver noise figures.
8. **High Network Congestion (Gradual Buildup):** Steady accumulation of user traffic exceeding PRB allocation capacity at the O-DU scheduler.
9. **High Network Congestion (Sudden Spike):** Abrupt bursts of network traffic causing instantaneous PRB saturation and transmission queue spikes.
10. **Jamming:** Intentional or unintentional external Over-the-Air (OTA) radio jamming across the operating channel bandwidth.
11. **Resource Allocation Bugs:** Scheduler software defects in token-bucket or QoS scheduling logic at the O-DU MAC layer.

### 2.4 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
Introduced by Lewis et al. [1], RAG pairs parametric sequence-to-sequence language models with non-parametric dense vector retrievers. In specialized engineering domains, RAG mitigates generative hallucinations by grounding model responses in semantically relevant external passages. Utilizing bi-encoder architectures like Sentence-BERT [5], domain text is encoded into high-dimensional embedding spaces, indexed via Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs, and retrieved via cosine distance.

### 2.5 SHAP for Model Explainability
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), proposed by Lundberg and Lee [2], computes game-theoretic Shapley values to assign additive feature attribution scores to predictive features:
$$\phi_i(x) = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} [f(S \cup \{i\}) - f(S)]$$
In tree-based ensembles (CatBoost), TreeSHAP efficiently calculates local feature impact, determining whether a KPI pushed the prediction toward or away from a given class relative to baseline normal means.

### 2.6 G-Eval Framework
Evaluating natural language explanations without reference ground-truth text presents substantial methodological challenges. Standard n-gram overlap metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) correlate poorly with human judgment in specialized technical domains. Liu et al. [3] established **G-Eval**, an LLM-as-a-judge evaluation protocol that defines multi-dimensional rubrics with explicit evaluation criteria scored across Likert scales.

### 2.7 Gap Analysis
Prior literature on automated telecom network management focuses predominantly on black-box classification accuracy or unsupervised clustering. Existing generative AI applications in telecommunications typically rely on unconstrained foundation models that hallucinate standards clauses or offer generic advice unsuitable for NOC workflows. To date, no published framework integrates local SHAP feature attributions, vector ticket retrieval, and formal 3GPP clause verification into a unified, auditable diagnostic loop.

### 3.1 Dataset: TelecomTS
The study utilizes the open **TelecomTS dataset** [13], comprising telemetric recordings of simulated and real-world 5G NR link behaviors:
- **Total Windows:** 32,000 records
- **Class Breakdown:** 30,765 Normal windows (96.14%) and 1,235 Anomalous windows (3.86%)
- **Window Length:** 128 timesteps sampled at 10ms intervals (1.28 seconds of real-time traffic per window)
- **Channels:** 18 KPI channels (16 continuous numeric KPIs and 2 discrete protocol/direction indicators)
- **Train/Test Partitioning:** To prevent temporal auto-correlation leakage, a session-based partition is enforced:
  - **Train Split:** 25,688 total windows (935 anomalous, 24,753 normal)
  - **Test Split:** 6,312 total windows (300 anomalous, 6,012 normal)

### 3.3 Feature Engineering (Phase 2)
In Phase 2, the feature engineering pipeline was substantially expanded by Farida El Ghabary to capture non-stationary temporal dynamics and frequency-domain signatures:
- **Base Statistical Descriptors (64 features):** Mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum across 16 numeric KPI channels. These constitute the feature set for Stage 1 (Model 1).
- **Extended Descriptors (496 features total for Model 2):**
  - **Temporal Patch Statistics (256 features):** The 128-timestep window is segmented into 8 non-overlapping temporal patches (`patch0` through `patch7`), with patch-level means and variances computed across all channels.
  - **FFT Spectral Band Energies (80 features):** Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis extracts spectral power across 4 distinct frequency bands (`band1` through `band4`) plus total spectral energy for each channel.
  - **Temporal Gradients and Trends:** First-order differences, curvature (convexity), and S-shape saturation metrics.
  - **Cross-Channel Correlations:** Correlation coefficients between paired KPIs (e.g., `RSRP` vs. `DL_BLER`, `PRB_Utilization` vs. `TX_Bytes`).

### 3.4 Two-Stage CatBoost Cascade
To address severe class imbalance (3.86% anomaly rate), detection and diagnosis are split into a two-stage hierarchical cascade:
- **Stage 1 (Binary Detector — Model 1):** CatBoostClassifier trained on 4,675 session-split rows using 64 base statistical features with 200 trees. Model 1 acts as a high-sensitivity triage filter, screening out normal network operations.
- **Stage 2 (Multi-Class Classifier — Model 2):** CatBoostClassifier trained on 5,185 session-split rows using 496 temporal, spectral, and statistical features with 1,000 trees across 11 fault classes. Stage 2 executes only on windows flagged by Stage 1.

### 3.5 SHAP Attribution
For every anomalous window, TreeSHAP computes Shapley attribution values for all 496 features. The top-3 features by absolute magnitude $|\phi_i|$ are extracted to form the `shap_top3` signature. Each entry encapsulates:
- The KPI channel and specific feature name.
- The signed SHAP value $\phi_i$ indicating magnitude and attribution.
- The attribution effect (`toward_predicted_class` vs. `away_from_predicted_class`).
- The empirical deviation direction relative to the pre-computed normal baseline: `feature_vs_normal` (`above_normal_mean` vs. `below_normal_mean`).

### 3.6 RAG Pipeline
The RAG retrieval corpus is constructed strictly from the **935 anomalous tickets belonging to the training split**:
- **Text Representation:** Each ticket concatenates the engineer's raw incident description (`ticket_text`) and the structured troubleshooting dialogue (`qna_trace`).
- **Embedding Model:** `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` produces 384-dimensional dense semantic vectors.
- **Vector Database:** ChromaDB configured with an approximate nearest neighbors HNSW index utilizing **cosine distance** space.
- **Query Construction:** Structured queries incorporate the predicted fault name, top SHAP KPI channels, and empirical deviation directions.
- **Retrieval Policy:** Top-$k = 5$ candidate documents are retrieved; tickets meeting the cosine threshold of $\tau = 0.35$ are formatted into the prompt context.

### 3.7 3GPP Alignment Table
Developed by Rodina Khallaf, the **3GPP Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table** (v1.1) formally maps TelecomTS fault classifications to international telecom standards. The table indexes 11 rows:
- 10 fault types have direct, authoritative references across 3GPP Release 15/16/17 specifications (e.g., TS 38.141-1 for conducted RF testing, TS 38.321 for MAC protocol operations, TS 28.552 for 5G performance measurements).
- **Jamming Exception:** Intentional RF jamming is classified as an external over-the-air event without a dedicated 3GPP conformance testing clause (`relation_type: "not-applicable"`, `3gpp_reference: null`). Jamming is therefore excluded from standards citation ablations and handled via ticket retrieval only.

### 3.8 RCA Evidence Integration
A curated repository of 289 window-indexed RCA records (`rca_evidence.json`) bridges data observations and standards. Each record contains:
1. `layer_a_observational`: Observed signal statistics during the event.
2. `layer_b_model_attribution`: Top-3 SHAP features and directions.
3. `standards_evidence`: Prescribed 3GPP clause, verbatim clause text, O-RAN architectural localization, and domain causal mechanism.
4. `kpi_evidence`: Cross-comparison categorizing observed KPIs into `strong` (domain-expected, degraded, and high SHAP), `supporting`, or `unexpected`.

### 3.9 LLM Backend
Generative explanations are produced via Google's `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` model:
- Configured with low temperature ($T = 0.1$) to enforce deterministic, fact-grounded reasoning.
- Native structured JSON mode (`response_mime_type: "application/json"`).
- Adaptive rate limiter maintaining a minimum 2.0s inter-request interval, coupled with exponential backoff on HTTP 429/quota limits (max 3 retries).
- Fallback backends supported in the codebase include Groq (`llama-3.1-8b-instant`) and Ollama (`llama3.1:8b`).

### 3.10 Evaluation Framework
The project implements a tripartite evaluation architecture:
- **Track A (ML Metrics):** Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Confusion Matrices for binary anomaly detection and 11-class fault classification.
- **Track B (Explanation Quality — G-Eval):** Multi-dimensional rubric assessing Citation Validity, Fault Specificity, Actionability, and Causal Soundness on a 1–5 scale.
- **Track C (Grounding Ablation):** Controlled 30-window ablation across three experimental conditions (C1, C2, C3) measuring automated citation accuracy against authoritative standards.

### 4.2 Data Pipeline
Implemented in [`src/data_loader.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/data_loader.py), the ingestion engine parses raw nested JSONL files. The function `filter_anomalous()` separates normal traces from failure events. Crucially, `apply_train_split()` reads `data/indices/train_idx_sessionsplit.npy` and verifies that only training windows enter the vector database, eliminating any possibility of data contamination.

### 4.3 ML Pipeline
Defined in [`src/ml_model.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/ml_model.py), the inference engine manages model loading and two-stage scoring. Inputs are validated against Pydantic schemas in [`src/schema.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/schema.py). `ClassifierOutput` encapsulates:

- `signal_statistics`: Dictionary of per-channel observed statistical moments.

### 4.4 RAG Pipeline
Vector storage is managed by [`src/kg_indexer.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/kg_indexer.py) and query execution by [`src/rag_query.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/rag_query.py). The function `query_from_classifier_output()` translates the output of Layer 2 into an optimized text query:
```
"Antenna Failure anomaly detected. Primary affected KPIs: RSRP, DL_BLER, DL_MCS. 
Signal direction: RSRP below normal mean. Protocol state: UDP."
```
Tickets are returned alongside their cosine similarities. If the maximum similarity falls below $\tau = 0.35$, a `low_confidence = True` flag is raised to caution downstream consumers.

### 4.5 LLM Explanation Pipeline
The reasoning engine in [`src/llm_explainer.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/llm_explainer.py) prompts the language model to synthesize observational evidence, historical tickets, and 3GPP clauses into a strict Pydantic model (`LLMExplanation`):
```json
{
  "root_cause": "Physical antenna feeder degradation causing sharp RSRP collapse.",
  "gpp_reference": "TS 38.141-1 §4.2.1",
  "oran_component": "O-RU",
  "recommended_action": "Inspect physical RF feeder cables and transceiver ports at O-RU.",
  "reasoning_trace": "RSRP was observed at -108.5 dBm (SHAP=-0.55), driving MCS collapse...",
  "reference_valid": true,
  "template_generated": false
}
```
Before delivery, `validate_citation()` performs a two-tier verification:
1. **Regex Format Verification:** Confirms valid 3GPP series format (`TS/TR XX.XXX`).
2. **Alignment Table Cross-Check:** Verifies that the cited specification matches the valid standard(s) pre-registered for that specific fault type. If a model hallucinates an unrelated specification, `reference_valid` is flagged `False`.

### 4.6 RCA Loader
Implemented in [`src/rca_loader.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/src/rca_loader.py), `RCALoader` constructs an in-memory $O(1)$ index over `data/processed/rca_evidence.json`. The method `get_prompt_context(window_index)` formats a concise, token-efficient evidence block (<400 tokens) detailing observed anomalies, model attribution, and clause evidence for injection into the C3 prompt.

### 4.7 Dashboard (Streamlit)
The web interface ([`dashboard/app.py`](file:///C:/00-Shobaki/Data%20Science/Queen%27s%20MCs/Graduation%20Project/project/dashboard/app.py)) provides an operational cockpit for NOC engineers across 6 specialized panels:
1. **Detection Panel:** Displays the predicted fault type, severity indicator, and model confidence.
2. **SHAP Panel:** Interactive waterfall/bar chart displaying the top-3 contributing KPIs with normal mean comparative baselines.
3. **RCA Panel:** Summarizes physical signal observations, expected vs. observed KPI agreement, and architectural fault localization.
4. **Explanation Panel:** Displays the structured LLM diagnosis, recommended remediation action, and full reasoning trace.
5. **Sources Panel:** Renders retrieved historical tickets from ChromaDB with cosine similarity scores and troubleshooting resolutions.
6. **Citation Panel:** Provides visual audit badges verifying 3GPP specification validity and linking to relevant technical clauses.

### 4.8 Key Technical Decisions
- **Cosine Metric Migration:** During Phase 2, ChromaDB was migrated from Euclidean (L2) distance to Cosine distance (`telecomts_tickets_cosine`). The earlier L2 distance distorted semantic similarity formulas ($1.0 - d$), causing negative similarity scores; cosine space guarantees bounded similarities $\in [0, 1]$.
- **Degenerate Calibrator Deprecation:** Diagnostic inspection of `model1_calibrator.pkl` revealed that its IsotonicRegression mapping collapsed all input probabilities between $0.30$ and $0.9967$ to a flat $0.0850$ ($8.5\%$), directly contradicting empirical test precision ($69.6\%$). The calibrator was safely disabled in `src/ml_model.py` in favor of raw probabilities.
- **Session-Based Data Hygiene:** Random k-fold cross-validation suffers severe data leakage in time-series telemetries due to sliding window overlap. Session-based splitting ensures test windows originate from entirely unseen network sessions.

### 5.4 Track B — G-Eval Quality Assessment
To evaluate qualitative explanation quality beyond citation string matching, the project implemented the G-Eval framework. Scoring rubrics assess four orthogonal dimensions on a 1.0–5.0 continuous Likert scale:
- **Citation Validity (CV):** Formal correctness and applicability of the 3GPP reference.
- **Fault Specificity (FS):** Accuracy in characterizing the specific fault mechanism and KPI degradation.
- **Actionability (AC):** Technical clarity and feasibility of the recommended remediation steps.
- **Causal Soundness (CS):** Consistency of the cause $\to$ symptom $\to$ KPI reasoning chain.

The composite score is computed via the pre-registered weighted formula prioritizing auditability:
$$\text{Composite Score} = \frac{2 \times \text{CV} + \text{FS} + \text{AC} + \text{CS}}{5}$$

| G-Eval Dimension | Metric Focus | Target Threshold | Pilot Evaluation Status |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|
| **Citation Validity** | Accurate 3GPP standard & clause | $\ge 4.0$ / 5.0 | **100% Pass** in C3 ($5.0/5.0$) |
| **Fault Specificity** | KPI signature and subsystem localization | $\ge 3.5$ / 5.0 | High ($>4.0$) across physical faults |
| **Actionability** | Feasible, standards-aligned NOC actions | $\ge 3.5$ / 5.0 | Moderate ($3.8–4.2$) |
| **Causal Soundness** | Mechanistic reasoning chain | $\ge 3.5$ / 5.0 | Sensitive to traffic vs. RF distinction |
| **Composite Score** | Weighted multi-dimensional metric | **$\ge 70.0\%$ (3.5/5.0)** | **Meets Pre-Registered Threshold** |

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Interpretation of Results
The experimental results conclusively validate the project's core hypothesis: Large Language Models, when applied in zero-shot or few-shot configurations without external standards grounding, cannot be trusted to generate auditable telecom diagnoses. 
The C1 citation accuracy of **27%** highlights that foundation LLMs carry fragmentary, often inaccurate recollections of 3GPP specification numbers. More importantly, C2 (**23%**) reveals that traditional ticket-based RAG—while beneficial for operational context—does not resolve citation hallucinations. Grounding via a dedicated **Alignment Table (C3 = 100%)** proves that domain knowledge must be structured and explicitly injected into the prompt context to achieve deterministic standards compliance.

### 6.2 G-Eval Analysis
Qualitative evaluation of generated diagnoses reveals important behavioral patterns across dimensions:
- **Causal Soundness Nuance:** Causal soundness scored lowest when diagnosing bursty transport anomalies (e.g., *High Network Congestion Sudden Spike*). The LLM occasionally conflated PRB saturation with physical layer RF degradation (e.g., attributing throughput collapse to antenna pathloss rather than transport queue congestion).
- **Actionability Depth:** Recommended actions proved highly actionable at the subsystem and architectural level (e.g., instructing engineers to inspect O-RU feeder connections or reconfigure O-DU scheduler token buckets), but occasionally lacked specific numeric threshold guidelines (e.g., recommending a specific dBm threshold).

### 6.3 Key Findings
1. **The Vector Metric Hazard:** Early Phase 1 iterations mistakenly instantiated ChromaDB using Euclidean (L2) distance while applying a cosine similarity transformation ($1 - d$), producing distorted negative similarities. Resolving this via explicit HNSW cosine metric configuration restored true similarity metrics.
2. **Isotonic Calibrator Degeneracy:** Farida's `model1_calibrator.pkl` exhibited catastrophic pooling collapse, flattening all probabilities between 0.30 and 0.9967 to 8.5%. Identifying this bug through empirical probing prevented the deployment of misleadingly suppressed confidence scores in production.
3. **Dual Role of SHAP:** Beyond human explainability, local SHAP values serve as an essential feature-reduction mechanism for RAG query construction, ensuring that only actively degraded KPIs drive vector search.
4. **Boundary of 3GPP Grounding:** Jamming demonstrated that not all operational network anomalies correspond to standardized 3GPP conformance testing clauses. Recognizing Jamming as an external event handled via ticket retrieval preserves the technical integrity of the standards alignment table.

### 6.4 Whether Objectives Were Met
| Objective | Target | Achieved Status | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Fault Detection** | Recall $\ge 85\%$ | **89.7%** (266/300) | Stage 1 CatBoost cascade |
| **Fault Classification** | Accuracy $\ge 65\%$ | **66.3%** exact, **75.0%** family | Stage 2 CatBoost (496 features) |
| **Standards Grounding** | Citation Validity $\ge 70\%$ | **100.0%** (30/30 in C3) | Track C ablation study ($\Delta = +77\text{pp}$) |
| **Operational Interface** | Interactive UI | **Live Dashboard** | Streamlit app with 6 active panels |
| **Software Reliability** | Full test coverage | **175 passing tests** | Automated CI regression suite |

### 6.5 Limitations
- **Sample Size ($n = 30$ per condition):** While the Wilson 95% confidence intervals for C2 [11.8%, 40.4%] and C3 [88.6%, 100.0%] do not overlap, expanding the sample size to hundreds of windows across multiple model seeds would provide tighter statistical bounds.
- **Congestion Class Scarcity in Test Split:** Phase 2 Model 2 exhibited very conservative predictions for congestion faults in the test session (2 gradual, 1 spike), requiring supplementation from the handoff file for Track C stratification.
- **Single-Rater Human Grounding:** The alignment table was curated primarily by one domain researcher (Rodina Khallaf) and validated against technical specifications; multi-expert consensus reviews will strengthen future releases.

### 6.6 Practical Implications
For telecommunications operators and NOC engineers, KPI-RAG transforms reactive alarm handling:
- **Instant Standards Auditing:** Every alarm is accompanied by the exact 3GPP specification clause governing the measurement, eliminating hours of manual documentation search.
- **Explainable Model Triage:** Rather than presenting an opaque classification label, the SHAP panel reveals the physiological reason for the alarm.
- **Reduced MTTR:** Coupling verified root causes with concrete O-RAN localized action items allows field engineers to dispatch repair crews directly to the affected unit (O-RU, O-DU, or Near-RT RIC).

## 7. Conclusion and Future Work

### 7.1 Main Achievements
The KPI-RAG project successfully demonstrated that:
1. Machine learning telemetric classifiers can be effectively coupled with generative language models without sacrificing technical auditability.
2. A two-stage CatBoost cascade on 496 temporal and spectral features achieves robust anomaly triage (89.7% recall) and multi-class diagnosis (66.3% exact / 75.0% family accuracy).
3. The novel 3GPP Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table resolves generative hallucinations, raising citation accuracy from 23% to 100%.

5. The entire codebase is hardened with 175 passing automated tests.

- **Domain Artifact:** Creation of the 5G Fault-to-Standard Alignment Table mapping 11 network failure modes to formal 3GPP TS clauses and O-RAN components.

### 7.3 Future Work

- **Extended Clause Granularity:** Expand the alignment table to include multi-clause hierarchical trees covering Release 18 (5G-Advanced).
- **Autonomous Mitigation via O-RAN rApps:** Integrate the explanation pipeline with Non-Real-Time RIC rApps to execute automated closed-loop remediation actions (e.g., dynamic PRB reallocation or automated tilt adjustment).
