# Research round 2 — algorithms likely to beat QUANT / tree ensembles on TelecomTS Task A (and help Task B)

Scope: web research only (2026-10-13). Setting recap: 935 labelled windows, 16 ch x 128 steps, 11 classes, session-grouped CV, CPU-only (256 cores). Current best: QUANT macro-F1 0.428 (+0.08 with fault-effect transplantation). Items marked [UNVERIFIED] were not confirmed from a primary source; [estimate] = my own CPU-runtime extrapolation, not a measured figure.

## 1. Time-series classifiers not yet tried

**Bake-off redux (Middlehurst, Schäfer, Bagnall, DMKD 2024; arXiv 2304.13029)** — 142 UCR datasets, 30 resamples, univariate.
- Mean accuracy on 112 UCR: HC2 0.891, MR-Hydra 0.884, MultiROCKET 0.881, RDST 0.876, H-InceptionTime 0.876, WEASEL 2.0 0.874, InceptionTime 0.874, MiniROCKET 0.874, Hydra 0.870, QUANT 0.867, FreshPRINCE 0.855, PF 0.837. https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13029v3
- By train-set size (avg rank, 142 sets): HC2 is #1 for 1-99 (2.70) and 100-299 (2.81) training cases; MR-Hydra #1 for 300-699 and 700+. For 11+ classes MR-Hydra #1 (2.50). Authors note HC2 does worse on imbalanced data. Same URL.
- Total CPU time over all 142 problems (Xeon Gold 5220R): QUANT 0.22 h, MR-Hydra 1.33 h, WEASEL 2.0 1.92 h, RDST 4.63 h, FreshPRINCE 30 h, HC2 264 h (median 15 min/dataset), PF 743 h. InceptionTime ran on GPU (no CPU figure). Same URL.
- Key sentence: "The most accurate classifiers we find all form homogeneous or heterogeneous ensembles, or extract features prior to a linear ridge classifier." Same URL.
- Implication for us: RDST is the one cheap classifier that outranks QUANT on UCR and is not yet tested; HC2 is the strongest in the n<300-per-fold regime but costly; WEASEL 2.0 is univariate-only in aeon (would need per-channel concatenation).
- aeon RDSTClassifier: multivariate supported, RidgeClassifierCV + StandardScaler head, n_jobs, `use_prime_dilations=True` for speed. https://www.aeon-toolkit.org/en/stable/api_reference/auto_generated/aeon.classification.shapelet_based.RDSTClassifier.html
- aeon WEASEL_V2: capability table says Multivariate: No. https://www.aeon-toolkit.org/en/stable/api_reference/auto_generated/aeon.classification.dictionary_based.WEASEL_V2.html
- QUANT paper (arXiv 2308.00928): whole 142-dataset archive in <15 min on one core; "same accuracy on average as the most accurate interval methods." https://github.com/angus924/quant

**RocketPFN (arXiv 2606.21786, June 2026)** — ROCKET features -> TabPFN v2.5 in-context classifier, no training.
- 92 UCR datasets: RocketPFN 0.900 = HC2 0.900 (p=0.50); beats every individual HC2 component; HC1 0.891, DrCIF 0.884, ROCKET 0.882, InceptionTime 0.880. 20 UEA multivariate: 0.747 vs HC2 0.747 (p=0.37), significantly better than plain ROCKET (p=0.033). With the same TabPFN head, ROCKET features (G=1, 200 features) 0.874 beat Mantis 0.859, MantisV2 0.851, MOMENT 0.845 (p<0.001). Median 30.6 s/fold on an L40S GPU (3.7 s for G=1). https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21786
- Independent re-run (AIMultiple, Aug 2026, 15 univariate datasets, frozen protocol): HC2 mean rank 2.40 / acc 0.898; RocketPFN 2.43 / 0.905 (highest accuracy); MultiRocket 0.890; Hydra 0.877; MiniRocket 0.876; MOMENT probe 0.853; Chronos-2 probe 0.835; TiRex probe 0.826; catch22 0.806; Mantis-V2 0.787. https://aimultiple.com/time-series-classification
- TabPFN practicalities: `pip install tabpfn`; README: on CPU "only small datasets (<~1000 samples) are feasible" — our 935 fits; TabPFN-2 weights under Prior Labs licence (Apache-2.0 + attribution), 2.5+ weights are non-commercial and need a token. https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/priorlabs/tabpfn@main/README.md , https://pypi.org/project/tabpfn/ . TabPFN v2 limit ~10k rows / 500 features [secondary source] https://www.beri.net/article/tabular-time-series-foundation-models-replace-xgboost-arima-2026
- Why this matters here: the RocketPFN recipe (random-conv or QUANT features, split into feature groups, average TabPFN probabilities across groups) directly replaces "features -> ridge/ExtraTrees" with an in-context learner tuned for n<1000 tabular problems.

**Deep learning on CPU**: InceptionTime/H-InceptionTime ~500k params and 0.874-0.876 on UCR (bake-off redux); the original paper trained 1,500 series in ~1 h on a Quadro P6000 GPU. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04939 . For 935 x 16 x 128 the per-epoch cost is tiny, so a 5-member ensemble is feasible on 256 cores in tens of minutes per fold [estimate], but there is no small-data/cross-session evidence that it beats MR-Hydra/HC2.

**Self-supervised pretraining on the 25k unlabelled windows**
- ESANN 2026 (Napoli & Borin), DAGHAR leave-one-dataset-out HAR, 4 backbones incl. TS2Vec: TF-C self-supervised initialisation "provides the largest and most consistent gains, improving mean accuracy by almost 2% across all backbones" (ERM random init 65.68 -> TF-C init 66.77, p<0.001); MixStyle -0.39% on average; DIFEX (objective-level DG) -3.14%. https://www.esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/2026/ES2026-193.pdf
- ESANN 2025 (same authors): TS2Vec + MixStyle best; MixStyle 0.67 vs ERM 0.62 mean accuracy, significant (p<=0.027). https://esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/2025/ES2025-135.pdf
- TF-C (NeurIPS 2022): +15.4% F1 average in one-to-one fine-tuning transfer. https://zitniklab.hms.harvard.edu/publications/papers/TF-C-self_supervised_contrastive_main.pdf
- Caveat: TS-CoT reports TS2Vec/TS-TCC "perform poorly when the label size becomes small" (10% labels). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.12551
- Net: SSL-init helps cross-domain by ~1-2 pts for deep nets, but no evidence it lifts a deep net above a strong feature-based ensemble at n=935.

## 2. Domain-shift / cross-recording tricks

- **Absolute scale matters on TelecomTS itself**: the TelecomTS paper (ICML 2026, arXiv 2510.06063) reports that adding a scale-aware branch "improved root cause accuracy across all architectures tested" (Autoformer up to +30.4 pts); Mantis RCA 0.590 with scaling vs 0.525 without; AD F1 0.800 vs 0.692. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06063v3 . So any per-window/per-recording normalisation must keep a raw-scale view alongside the normalised one.
- **Test-time normalisation (OFTTA, cross-person HAR, arXiv 2310.18562)**: replacing BN statistics with an exponentially-decayed mix of source and test-batch statistics + prototype classifier, no gradients: ERM 74.71 -> OFTTA 78.59 avg accuracy (+3.88) across UCI-HAR/OPPO/UniMiB; TENT 76.87, T3A 76.55. https://arxiv.org/html/2310.18562v2 . Analogue for us: causal running mean/std per recording as the normalisation reference (Task B).
- **DIVERSIFY (ICLR 2023)** cross-person HAR (DSADS/USC-HAD/PAMAP, 2-block CNN): ERM 75.7, DANN 77.7, CORAL 75.7, Mixup 78.6, GroupDRO 76.5, DIVERSIFY 81.0 avg. Cross-dataset: ERM 33.3 -> DIVERSIFY 51.1. Training cost ~357 s vs ERM 306 s (Mixup 2043 s). Code: microsoft/robustlearn. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07027
- **Mixup/CutMix on UEA (Yang & Desell 2022, 26 UEA sets, InceptionTime)**: isolated cutout ~+1.1 pt, mixup ~+0.8, window-warp ~+0.2, cutmix ~+0.1, all four ~+1.9 (16/26 datasets improved); up to +48 pts on EthanolConcentration. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.11739 . Mix-based aug. on 6 physiological datasets "consistently improve". https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09970
- **Adaptive input normalisation (EDAIN, AISTATS 2024)**: learned per-instance shift/scale/outlier layer beats z-score/DAIN on classification (credit default, LOB). https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/september24a.html — NN-only.
- **EEG DG benchmark (SEED)**: Mixup ~0.74 vs ERM ~0.64 with ResNet-18. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09016
- **Rank/quantile encoding of features under shift**: no primary study found; sklearn docs only note out-of-range test values are clipped to bounds. https://scikit-learn.org/1.3/modules/generated/sklearn.preprocessing.quantile_transform.html [UNVERIFIED that it helps cross-recording]
- **Domain-adversarial (DANN)**: +2.0 pts over ERM cross-person in DIVERSIFY table (above); DIFEX negative in ESANN 2026. Weak/inconsistent evidence; not recommended over simpler normalisation.

## 3. Foundation models on CPU (frozen embeddings)

- **Mantis-8M / MantisV2** (`pip install mantis-tsfm`, Apache-2.0, HF `paris-noah/Mantis-8M`, `paris-noah/MantisV2`; ViT, 8.1M / 4.2M params, input interpolated to 512, per-channel encoding + concat; README says fine-tuning "even feasible on a CPU"). https://github.com/vfeofanov/mantis , https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15637v1
  - MantisV2 (arXiv 2602.17868) zero-shot (frozen + LogReg) on cross-subject-style benchmarks: HAR avg MantisV2 0.756, Mantis+ 0.746, NuTime 0.738, TiRex 0.699, Chronos2 0.685, MOMENT 0.679, Catch22+ 0.648; EEG avg MantisV2 0.736, Chronos2 0.724, MOMENT 0.702, Catch22+ 0.697. Test-time tricks: layer-3 features, CLS+mean token concat, multi-length self-ensemble (128/256/512/1024 + first difference). https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17868v1
  - On TelecomTS (random 80/20, balanced): Mantis frozen = best AD F1 0.800; RCA accuracy 0.590 (Toto 0.848, TimesNet 0.685, Informer 0.654, MOMENT 0.550). https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06063v3
  - But with an identical TabPFN head, ROCKET features beat Mantis/MantisV2/MOMENT (0.874 vs 0.859/0.851/0.845, p<0.001). https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21786
  - CPU runtime: 25k windows x 16 channels = 400k univariate passes through an 8M ViT at length 512; ~1-3 h on 256 cores [estimate]; 935 windows: seconds-minutes.
- **Forecasting FMs as frozen extractors (Auer et al., arXiv 2510.26777; RF head; mean-pool, per-variate concat)**: UEA multivariate accuracy TiRex 0.74, Chronos-Bolt-base 0.72 (0.74 with stats+diff aug), Mantis 0.74, Moirai-L 0.70, TimesFM-2 0.70, Chronos-base 0.71, MOMENT-L 0.57, DTW 0.72. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26777v1 . Chronos-Bolt sizes 9M/21M/48M/205M, "up to 250x faster" than Chronos, CPU deployment supported. https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-bolt-small , https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-deutsche-bahn-redefines-forecasting-using-chronos-models-now-available-on-amazon-bedrock-marketplace . StarEmbed benchmark: Chronos-Bolt first on all metrics vs hand-crafted features. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06200
- **MOMENT-1-small**: 37.9M params, `pip install momentfm`. https://huggingface.co/AutonLab/MOMENT-1-small . Multiple independent benchmarks place MOMENT below Mantis/Chronos-Bolt/TiRex on multivariate (0.57 UEA in Auer et al.; last on the AIMultiple multivariate panel).
- **Toto (Datadog, 151M)** got the best TelecomTS RCA (0.848) but under a random split; CPU inference ~10-20x Mantis cost [estimate]; multimodal Toto+Qwen 0.826. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06063v3
- Bottom line: frozen FM embeddings are worth adding as extra feature blocks (Mantis-8M, Chronos-Bolt-small), not as replacements; the strongest independent evidence says ROCKET/MiniRocket/HC2-type features still win on full-train benchmarks.

## 4. Ensembling evidence

- **CAWPE (Large, Lines, Bagnall, DMKD 2019)**: weighted probability average with weight = CV-accuracy^4 (alpha=4 fixed a priori). On 121 UCI sets, CAWPE beat the pick-best component on 46 (lost 18). Gain vs train size (CAWPE-S vs pick-best): 1-100 cases: 21/28 wins, +1.49% error; 101-500: 36/46, +0.71%; 501-1000: 11/12, +1.51%; 1001-5000: +0.16%; >5000: +0.02%. CAWPE beat majority vote (0.184 vs 0.195 error) and stacking (0.195-0.214). HIVE-COTE with CAWPE 87.16% vs weighted majority vote 85.97%. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6790343/
- **HIVE-COTE 2.0 (arXiv 2104.07551)**: "picking the best component on the train data is significantly worse than HC2"; on 31 datasets combining representations beats the best component even with hindsight. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07551
- Tabular (Maillet et al. 2026): robust soft voting "particularly successful, especially in the multiclass case"; blending/stacking inconsistent. https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/choosing-a-parallel-heterogeneous-ensemble [UNVERIFIED details]
- Verdict: for n<1000 and multiclass, a CV-accuracy-weighted soft vote (not stacking, not pick-best) is the evidence-backed choice. Our models already produce the needed OOF probabilities; MiniRocket/Hydra ridge outputs need calibration (ridge gives no probabilities — bake-off notes "weaker probability estimates" for linear-classifier pipelines).

## 5. TelecomTS / 5G / O-RAN small-label RCA papers (2025-2026)

- **TelecomTS (arXiv 2510.06063, ICML 2026)**: 11 anomaly types (10 injected + real jamming), 128-step windows stride 32, ~32k samples; baselines use random 80/20 split with class-balanced training, Adam 1e-3, 5 epochs, frozen-backbone heads for FMs; RCA: Toto 0.848, Toto+Qwen 0.826, TimesNet 0.685, Informer 0.654, Mantis 0.590, MOMENT 0.550; LLMs 0.12-0.28. Not session-grouped, so not comparable to our 0.43. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06063v3 , code https://github.com/Ali-maatouk/TelecomTS
- **DK-Root (arXiv 2511.11737, Nov 2025)**: 5G QoE RCA from ~37 KPIs, 40-step windows, 6 classes, 3,000 rule-labelled + 185 expert-labelled; CNN encoder + class-conditional DDPM augmentation + supervised contrastive pretraining then fine-tune: 0.683 acc vs SVM 0.611, Sliding-KNN 0.577, CA-TCC 0.513, TS-TCC 0.512, TSF 0.438. Semi-supervised deep baselines lost to SVM at this label count. No code. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11737v1
- **FALCON / FALCON++ (arXiv 2503.06197)**: O-RAN fault prediction treated as anomaly detection "because labeled fault data is scarce" (LSTM-AE on normal). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06197
- **Simba (arXiv 2406.15638)**: GNN+Transformer AD+RCA on 5G RAN KPIs, supervised. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15638
- **Classification of anomalies in telecom KPI time series (arXiv 2308.16279)**: classifiers trained on simulated anomalies transfer to real KPIs — supports your transplantation augmentation. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.16279
- **DAST (arXiv 2606.06261, 2026)**: zero-shot VLM/LLM pipeline on O-RAN testbed traces, F1 0.910 AD. https://papers.cool/arxiv/2606.06261 [UNVERIFIED details]
- **Causal intervention sequence analysis for RAN fault tracking (arXiv 2511.17505)**: unsupervised. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17505v1
- Nothing found that uses TelecomTS with session-grouped evaluation; your protocol is stricter than the paper's.

## Ranked top-5 to try next (excluding everything already tested)

1. **CV-accuracy-weighted soft-vote ensemble (CAWPE, alpha=4) over QUANT + CatBoost(catch22) + LightGBM + RDST + MR-Hydra (calibrated).** pip: nothing new (aeon already; sklearn CalibratedClassifierCV for ridge members). Evidence: CAWPE wins 75-90% of problems under 1000 training cases and beats pick-best/stacking/majority vote; HC2 shows pick-best is significantly worse. Expected: +0.02 to +0.05 macro-F1 over 0.428. CPU: minutes — reuse existing OOF fold probabilities; inner-CV weights per outer fold.
2. **RocketPFN-style TabPFN v2 head on QUANT / MiniRocket features.** `pip install tabpfn` (TabPFN-2 weights, Apache-style licence; CPU OK for <=1000 rows). Split features into groups of <=500, run TabPFN per group, average probabilities (exactly RocketPFN's G-group trick). Evidence: ties HC2 on UCR (0.900) and UEA (0.747), beats ROCKET-ridge, beats FM embeddings with same head; TabPFN built for n<10k. Expected: +0.02 to +0.06 over QUANT+ExtraTrees. CPU: ~1-5 min per group per fold at 935 x 500 [estimate]; 25 folds x ~4 groups -> 2-8 h total.
3. **Dual-view features: causal per-recording normalisation (EDTN/OFTTA-style running stats) + raw absolute-scale view, fed to QUANT/CatBoost.** No pip. Evidence: TelecomTS paper shows scale-aware branch is the single largest lever on this dataset (+ up to 30 pts); OFTTA test-time normalisation +3.9 pts cross-person without gradients. Expected: Task A +0.01 to +0.04; Task B false-alarm reduction is the main target. CPU: negligible.
4. **Mantis-8M / MantisV2 frozen embeddings (+ Chronos-Bolt-small) as extra feature blocks into (1)/(2).** `pip install mantis-tsfm` (and `chronos-forecasting`). Use MantisV2 test-time tricks (layer-3, CLS+mean, multi-length self-ensemble incl. first difference). Evidence: best frozen FM on HAR/EEG cross-subject benchmarks (0.756 vs MOMENT 0.679); best AD F1 (0.800) on TelecomTS itself; Chronos-Bolt 0.72-0.74 UEA with RF. Expected: +0.01 to +0.04 (complementary, not standalone). CPU: 25k x 16 ch -> ~1-3 h for Mantis-8M, similar for Bolt-small [estimate]; 935 windows: minutes.
5. **RDST and (contracted) HIVE-COTE 2 as additional members / standalone checks.** `pip install aeon` (already). Evidence: RDST 0.876 vs QUANT 0.867 on UCR at 4.6 CPU-h for the whole archive; HC2 #1 for train sizes <300 and 0.891 overall, but weaker on imbalanced data and 264 CPU-h archive-wide. Expected: RDST +0.00 to +0.03 standalone, more inside (1); HC2 +0.01 to +0.04 but mainly as a probability source. CPU: RDST ~1-5 min/fold with n_jobs=-1 [estimate]; HC2 ~0.5-2 h/fold on 256 cores, use `time_limit_in_minutes` [estimate].

Runner-up (higher variance): TF-C or TS2Vec pretraining on the 25k unlabelled windows, then fine-tune a small CNN with mixup/cutout and MixStyle — evidence of +1-2 pts cross-domain for deep nets (ESANN 2026, DIVERSIFY Mixup +2.9), but no evidence a deep net beats feature ensembles at n=935 (DK-Root: SVM beat TS-TCC/CA-TCC at 185 labels).
